**Short answer: no. The check you ran does not validate the simulator yet, so the 91% is indicative and gates nothing.**

1. **A gap inside the tolerance is not a pass; the whole interval must be.** The 50 cards are paired with the real conversations they came from, so read the gap as a paired difference. 9 of 50 pairs disagree (d = 18%), so the 95% interval is about ±1.96 × √(0.18 / 50) ≈ ±12 points: the +6 gap runs from about −6 to +18. The upper end is well past your ±8 tolerance, so the simulator may inflate the pass rate by more than you accept.
2. **Size the cards for the tolerance minus the gap.** With a 6-point gap, the whole interval stays inside ±8 only if its half-width is 2 points or less: 1.96² × 0.18 / 0.02² ≈ 1,700 cards. About 110 cards (1.96² × 0.18 / 0.08²) is enough only if the true gap is near zero. So fix the simulator first, since the gap leans toward too cooperative, then run a fresh comparison of about 110 cards; or widen the tolerance and write the wider one down before the next comparison.
3. **Length on the average is not enough.** Compare the median and the 90th percentile of customer messages, and add the rows that make a dispute hard: share of vague first messages, share with a user correction, facts given only when asked (the hidden fact: a card lent to a relative, a subscription forgotten), abandonment.
4. **The direction matters.** The simulated customers resolve more often than real ones (88% against 82%): simulated customers are too cooperative, so the 91% likely overstates what real customers get.
5. **Then gate.** Once the simulator passes, gate per conversation over 3 runs per scenario with the observed pass^3, bars signed before the run. Until then, gate on scripted cases replayed from the real logs.
