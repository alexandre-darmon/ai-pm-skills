**Short version:** the slide shouldn't go as written. "0% on 200" is a tuning result, not a robustness rate, and a model risk committee will probably see that quickly. The fixes are mostly about what you claim, plus one test you should run before Thursday.

## What's wrong with the slide

**1. The 200 attacks were used to tune the defence.** The team iterated on the prompt and filter until none of the 200 got through, so 0/200 measures how well the defence fits those attacks. It says little about attacks it hasn't seen. A rate for a decision has to come from attacks nobody tuned against.

**2. Zero is not 0%.** With 0 successes in 200 independent attacks, the true rate could still be about 1.5% (rule of three). The Wilson 95% upper bound is about **1.9%**. Even on a clean sample, the defensible statement is "under about 2%", not "0%".

**3. The 200 are probably not 200 independent attacks.** If they include variants of the same idea, the effective n is lower and the bound is wider. Count seeds (distinct attack ideas), not variants.

**4. A pooled rate hides the classes that matter.** For a mortgage assistant, the harms differ a lot in severity. Examples are leaking another applicant's data or the system prompt, giving a binding rate or approval statement, bypassing fair-lending or disclosure rules, and manipulating the pre-qualification outcome. A pooled 0% can sit alongside a weak class, and the committee will ask which classes the 200 cover.

**5. Missing context.** The slide doesn't state:
- who wrote the attacks (the build team, which is not independent)
- the success criteria
- the model, temperature and number of attempts per attack
- what wasn't tested, such as indirect injection through uploaded documents, multi-turn attacks and tool or outbound paths, if any exist
- who accepts the residual risk

**6. "Release requested" shouldn't sit on a robustness claim.** Never conclude the system is robust or secure. The evidence supports a measured rate per class with its interval and a list of untested classes.

## What to change before Thursday

1. **Relabel the 200 honestly.** Call them a development/regression set, say they were used for tuning, and say 0/200 is the expected result of that. Keep them as the regression suite for every future change.

2. **Get a fresh held-out set.** Someone independent of the three builders, such as security, second line or an external tester, writes new attacks. Those attacks are sealed and never shown to the prompt engineer, and the defence is frozen at v1.4 before they run. Then report both numbers and the gap between them. A large gap means the defence learned the attacks rather than the class. If time is short, even 100–150 fresh seeds across the main classes is far more informative than the 200, but report it as indicative.

3. **Replace the headline with a per-class table.** Use this format:

   | Class | Successes / seeds | Rate (Wilson 95%) | Tolerance (signed beforehand) | Reading |
   |---|---|---|---|---|

   Each reading is under tolerance, over tolerance, or undecided. Undecided means "more attacks", not a looser reading. Classes to cover include direct injection, system-prompt and data exfiltration, policy bypass (rate or approval promises, fair-lending content), multi-turn attacks, and indirect injection if the assistant reads documents.

4. **Size the tolerances.** To claim a class is under 1% with zero successes, you need about 380 held-out seeds. For 0.5% you need about 765. For costly classes such as another customer's data, size for that class's own tolerance. A class below its n is reported as indicative.

5. **State the exposure.** Estimate monthly attack attempts from logs, not the 15,000 conversations. For example, at 150 attempts a month and a 1.9% bound, up to about 3 successes a month remain possible. Add the persistent attacker's view: at roughly 2%, a first success takes about 50 tries.

6. **Add the missing context.** For each class, list the testers and their independence, the settings and versions, and the date. Also list the classes not tested and why.

7. **Add a residual-risk line.** Name an owner outside the build team who accepts the remaining risk, and state what triggers a re-test: a model, prompt, filter or data-source change, or a date.

8. **Reword the claim.** Something like:

   > "On a development set of 200 attacks (used in tuning), v1.4 shows 0 successes. On an independent held-out set of N attacks across K classes, per-class attack-success rates and 95% intervals are shown below; all are/are not under the tolerances signed on [date]. Classes not tested: … Residual risk owner: … Re-test triggers: …"
