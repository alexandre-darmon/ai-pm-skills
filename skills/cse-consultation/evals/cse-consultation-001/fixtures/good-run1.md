# CSE memo: AI email-reply assistant at your bank

## Obligation level
| Obligation | Article | Applies? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consultation on a new technology | L2312-8, II, 4° | **Yes.** Generative AI counts as a new technology, even if use is optional (TJ Créteil, 15 July 2025, upheld by CA Paris, 21 May 2026). Drafting client replies also changes advisers' tasks and workload. |
| Consultation on means of monitoring activity | L2312-38 | **Probably yes.** See the monitoring screen below. |
| Prior information of employees | L1222-4 | Yes, before any data about them is collected. |
| Proportionality of any monitoring | L1121-1 | Yes, if the tool records individual activity. |

## Monitoring screen (to verify with your vendor)
Tools like this usually log prompts, drafts, edit rates, usage per adviser and time stamps. If the tool can record or report what an identified adviser does, the stricter path applies even if you never intend to use that capability. That path is consultation under L2312-38 (Cass. soc., 11 Dec. 2019, n° 18-11.792). Find out what is logged, who can see it, and whether it can be switched off.

## The pilot is the main risk
- A pilot where the target advisers use the tool in their real work counts as a first implementation, not an experiment (TJ Nanterre, 14 Feb. 2025). Only a limited test needed to build the CSE's file may come first.
- Consultation must precede the decision (L2312-14).
- Skipping it is the offence of obstruction (L2317-1): a fine of up to EUR 7,500, or EUR 37,500 for a company. A judge can also suspend the tool under penalty payments (Cass. soc., 27 Nov. 2024).

## Timeline (no company agreement on delays, R2312-6)
| Milestone | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| File given to the CSE or placed in the BDESE, and the CSE told | This starts the clock (R2312-5) |
| Opinion deadline | 1 month, or 2 months if the CSE appoints an expert (L2315-94, 2°; 80% paid by you, L2315-80) |
| Silence at the deadline | The CSE is deemed consulted, with a negative opinion (L2312-16) |
| Pilot with real advisers | Not before the opinion or the end of the delay |
| Full rollout | March 2027 |

If the file went in today (4 November), the earliest opinion would be around 4 December with no expert. With an expert it would be early January. A December pilot is therefore unrealistic unless it is a limited test, and it must not involve advisers using the tool on real client emails. Your options are:
1. **Recommended:** consult once on the whole project (pilot and rollout), then start the pilot after the opinion, possibly in January or February. March stays feasible.
2. Start only a narrow, closed test to build the file, with no real client work. HR and legal must confirm it qualifies.

If a company agreement sets other consultation delays, those apply instead.

## File for the CSE (L2312-15)
- Purpose and expected benefits.
- How the tool works, its limits and error cases, including who checks the drafts and who is accountable for what is sent.
- Data processed, retention and access. Client emails contain personal data and banking secrecy applies.
- Monitoring capabilities and safeguards.
- Impact on tasks, workload, pace, skills and health and safety. Examples are review fatigue, pressure to answer faster, and the risk of a deskilled advisory role.
- Training plan.
- Timeline and pilot scope (40 advisers, then 140).
- AI Act classification (`ai-act-classification`) and GDPR impact assessment (`dpia-prescreen`). The impact assessment is likely required, and CNIL guidance on employee monitoring applies.

## CSE presentation outline (plain language)
1. What the assistant does (drafts replies that the adviser edits and sends) and what it does not do (it never sends on its own).
2. What changes for advisers, and what does not.
3. What is recorded, who sees it and for how long. Say explicitly whether adviser-level statistics are produced or used for evaluation.
4. Safeguards, and how advisers can raise a concern.
5. Training and support.
6. Timeline and next steps.
