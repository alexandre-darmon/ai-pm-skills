# Employee representatives and AI tools (9 employees, Paris)

I applied the CSE consultation method from the skill. I didn't open its reference annex, so I haven't checked its `last_verified` date. Have HR or legal confirm the article references before you rely on them.

## 1. Obligations towards employee representatives: none for now

- A CSE (works council) is only mandatory once a company has had at least 11 employees for 12 consecutive months (L2311-2). At 9 employees you have no CSE, so you have no duty to inform or consult one.
- The duty to consult on new technologies (L2312-8) and on monitoring means (L2312-38) starts at 50 employees. Even a company with a CSE and 11 to 49 employees has no such duty (L2312-5).
- If you grow, the position changes. At 11 employees for 12 months you must set up a CSE. From 50, you must consult it before you deploy tools like these, and deploying without consulting is the offence of *délit d'entrave* (L2317-1). Document today's decision so you can show later how you handled it.

Having no CSE doesn't mean you have nothing to do. Three duties apply at any headcount.

## 2. What you must do before you start

1. **Tell employees before any data about them is collected (L1222-4).** This is the key legal duty for you. Both tools collect data about people:
   - The note-taker records voices and words, and the minutes attribute statements to individuals.
   - The copilot may log prompts, usage and output per person.
2. **Keep any restriction justified and proportionate (L1121-1).** Record the purpose, for example productivity and minutes. Don't use the tools for covert evaluation of individuals. Switch off any per-person usage reporting you don't need.
3. **Comply with GDPR.** The AI note-taker is the sensitive one:
   - Choose a legal basis, set a retention period, and make sure you have a data processing agreement with the vendor.
   - Check where the data is hosted and whether it is used to train models.
   - Add the tools to your processing register.
   - Run a DPIA pre-screen (the `dpia-prescreen` skill covers this).
   - Read the CNIL guidance on employee monitoring.

## 3. Practical steps, in order

1. **List what each tool records and who can see it.** Find out whether admins can see individual prompts or per-person stats, and whether you can turn those off.
2. **Write a short usage policy:** purpose, what is recorded, who has access, retention period, what is forbidden (for example no confidential client code in the copilot, and no recording of HR or one-to-one calls). Share it with the whole team and keep proof they received it.
3. **Hold a team discussion before the pilot.** Voluntary consultation isn't required, but it is good practice under 11 employees, and it lowers friction.
4. **Handle call participants.** For external participants such as clients, candidates and partners, announce the recording at the start of the call and offer a way to opt out. For candidates, the L1221-8 and L1221-9 information duties apply. Don't record calls that are sensitive (HR, disputes, health).
5. **Check client contracts** for confidentiality clauses that limit recording or AI processing.
6. **Check the AI Act classification** (the `ai-act-classification` skill covers this). For these uses it is probably limited-risk, but emotion detection or performance scoring would change that.
7. **Keep a record:** the decision date, the policy and the information sent to employees.

## 4. Questions for HR and legal

- Does the vendor's default configuration let managers see usage or transcripts per person?
- Will minutes be used in performance reviews? If so, the stricter proportionality and information duties apply.
- Are you likely to reach 11 or 50 employees within 12 months? If so, plan the CSE election and the future consultation file now.
