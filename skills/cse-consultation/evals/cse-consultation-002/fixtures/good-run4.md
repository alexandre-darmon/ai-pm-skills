**Short answer:** No. With 9 employees you have no obligation to inform or consult employee representatives. You still have legal duties towards the employees themselves, and the note-taker is the sensitive tool.

## Regime and obligation level
- A CSE (works council) is only mandatory once a company has had at least 11 employees for 12 consecutive months (L2311-2). At 9 employees you have none, so there is nothing to consult.
- The duty to inform and consult the CSE on new technologies (L2312-8) and on monitoring means (L2312-38) only applies from 50 employees. Even with a CSE of 11 to 49 people, it has limited attributions (L2312-5) and no consultation right on these topics.
- I'm assuming you have never had 11 or more employees. If you have, tell me, because the analysis changes.

## What you must still do before starting
1. **Tell employees before any data about them is collected** (L1222-4). That covers the note-taker's recordings and transcripts, and any per-person usage logs from either tool.
2. **Keep it proportionate** (L1121-1). Record only what you need and set a short retention period. Switch off per-person analytics, such as speaking time, scoring and sentiment. Many note-takers have those features, and having the capability is a risk even if you never use it.
3. **Handle the recording of calls.**
   - Tell every participant at the start of each call. This includes clients, candidates and other external people, who need a GDPR notice and a clear option to object.
   - Don't use the note-taker on one-to-one reviews, HR conversations or similar calls.
4. **Do the GDPR groundwork.**
   - Add both tools to your processing register.
   - Check the vendors' data processing agreements, hosting location (EU or not), and whether they train on your data.
   - Run a short DPIA pre-screen for the note-taker. Systematic recording of meetings is the higher-risk one.
   - Check CNIL guidance on employee monitoring.
5. **Check the AI Act basics.** Under Article 4, you must give staff a basic level of AI literacy. Avoid any note-taker feature that infers emotions at work, because that is prohibited.
6. **Protect your code and confidentiality.** Choose a copilot plan that doesn't retain or train on your code. Check client contracts for clauses that restrict sending their code or discussions to third parties.

## Good practice, though not required
- Hold a short team meeting and write a one-page usage charter. It should cover what is recorded, who can see it, how long it is kept, and when the note-taker must be off.
- Ask your staff for feedback and let people raise concerns. At your size this does the job a CSE would otherwise do.
- Reread the contracts of any employee who might object, and be careful about imposing the note-taker on people who didn't expect to be recorded.

## When this changes
- **11 employees for 12 consecutive months:** you must set up a CSE. It has limited attributions, and members can raise an alert if employee rights are infringed (L2312-59).
- **50 employees for 12 consecutive months:** you must inform and consult the CSE before deploying a new technology (L2312-8) or any means of monitoring employee activity (L2312-38). The consultation must come before the decision (L2312-14). A pilot with real users counts as deployment, and skipping the consultation is an offence (L2317-1).
- If you grow, design the setup now so it can pass that test later: per-person tracking off, a written file describing the tools, and minimal retention.
