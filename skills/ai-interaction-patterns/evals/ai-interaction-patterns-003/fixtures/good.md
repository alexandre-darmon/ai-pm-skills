Don't stop one-by-one validation on this figure. 97% acceptance at about 9 seconds a claim looks like rubber-stamping, not trust: 9 seconds is too short to read a claim file. It is an inference to confirm, not a fact.

1. **Test the handlers with seeded errors.** Seed 20 known-wrong recommendations into the queue over two weeks (withdrawn before anything goes out) and measure the catch rate. If most seeded errors pass, the review is not working.
2. **Test the tool with a blind re-review.** An expert re-decides a random sample of 300 accepted claims without seeing the recommendation. Zero errors in 300 still allows up to about 1% (rule of three). Report wrong approvals and wrong rejections apart.
3. **Change the screen, not the level.** Put the evidence beside the recommendation: the policy clause, the photos and the three facts that drove it, flagged mismatches first, so the check is possible in the time spent.
4. **No badge.** A green "high confidence" badge would speed up rubber-stamping. Show a confidence figure only once the score has been checked against real outcomes in a reliability table. Until then show sources, not a number.
5. **L3 later, behind its gate.** Moving to L3 needs the gate in the autonomy ladder: a shadow run of at least two weeks, a known guardrail false-positive rate, a tested escalation path and the audited error rate, not an acceptance rate.
6. **Rejections and the law.** Without a handler on each claim, a rejection may become a solely automated decision under GDPR art. 22. Questions for the DPO before any change: legal basis, how the customer obtains a human review and contests. Keep rejections at L2 until counsel answers.

Placeholders (20 seeded, 300 sampled, two weeks) are to be set from your own volumes.

Ways to say the same thing that must stay clean: 97% acceptance proves nothing about trust. The 97% acceptance shows over-trust, not trust. 97% acceptance in 9 seconds shows automation bias, not trust. The 97% shows speed, not trust. Management's claim that 97% proves trust does not hold. Switching to L3 on this evidence would be a mistake. Going to L3 today would make rejections solely automated. If the audit shows the tool is accurate for one claim type, you can drop one-by-one review there.
